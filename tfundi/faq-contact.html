What is POPIA and why does my practice need to comply?

The Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) is South African law that governs how organisations collect, store, process, and share personal information. It came into full effect on 1 July 2021 and applies to virtually every business in South Africa that handles personal data.

For law firms and medical practices, compliance is not optional. You process some of the most sensitive personal information in existence — legal files, financial records, medical histories, diagnoses — and POPIA creates specific legal obligations around how you protect it. Non-compliance can result in fines of up to R10 million per incident and potential criminal liability.

What happens if my practice is not POPIA compliant?

The Information Regulator of South Africa has the authority to issue enforcement notices, conduct investigations, and impose fines of up to R10 million per violation. In serious cases, directors and responsible parties can face criminal prosecution.

Beyond regulatory penalties, a client or patient data breach can expose your practice to civil claims, professional negligence proceedings, and significant reputational damage. Your compliance status is also now publicly visible on the CIPC BizPortal — clients and referral partners can check it.

What is an Information Officer and do I need one?

Yes. POPIA requires every organisation that processes personal information to designate and register an Information Officer with the Information Regulator. For most law firms and medical practices, this is typically a senior partner, the practice owner, or a practice manager.

TACFundi handles the registration process for you as part of our audit service — including the CIPC BizPortal registration that makes your compliance status publicly visible.

Many IT providers are not trained in POPIA requirements, data processing documentation, or breach notification obligations.

TACFundi complements your existing IT support. We handle the compliance, governance, and legal data protection requirements; your IT person continues handling the day-to-day technical support.

What client data is covered by POPIA at a law firm?

Under POPIA, personal information includes names, ID numbers, contact details, financial information, and any information that can identify an individual. For a law firm, this covers virtually all client data: matter files, correspondence, property transfer documents, financial records, divorce proceedings, estate files, and more.

POPIA also places specific obligations on how this data is stored, who can access it, how long it can be retained, and what must happen if it is compromised.

Does POPIA compliance affect how we use email?

Yes, significantly. Emailing sensitive client documents without encryption is one of the most common POPIA vulnerabilities we find at law firms. POPIA requires that personal information be protected with appropriate security measures during transmission — standard email does not meet this requirement for highly sensitive files.

TACFundi assesses your current email practices and implements appropriate secure document sharing solutions that work within your existing workflows — without requiring you or your clients to learn complex new tools.

Why does POPIA treat medical practices differently?

POPIA classifies health information as "special personal information" under Section 26 — a higher protection category than general personal data. This means medical practices face stricter requirements, stricter enforcement, and higher penalties for violations than most other business types.

The rationale is straightforward: a patient's medical history, diagnosis, and treatment records are among the most sensitive information that can exist. POPIA reflects this by requiring a higher standard of care from organisations that process it.

Does our practice management software need to be POPIA compliant?

The software itself needs to meet certain security standards, and your use of it needs to be properly governed. This includes ensuring that access is restricted to authorised staff, that patient data is not stored in insecure locations, and that you have a documented agreement with the software provider addressing their data processing obligations under POPIA.

TACFundi reviews your practice management software setup as part of our audit and advises on any gaps — without requiring you to change systems unless absolutely necessary.

My receptionist handles most of the patient data. What do they need to know?

Quite a lot — and our staff training is designed specifically for this. Receptionists and admin staff need to understand what patient information they can and cannot share over the phone, how to handle requests for patient records, what to do if they receive a suspicious email or call, and who to contact if they suspect something has gone wrong.

Our 90-minute training session is practical, scenario-based, and written in plain English. No technical background required. Staff receive a completion certificate for their employment records.

What happens if a patient asks us to delete their records?

POPIA grants individuals the right to request deletion of their personal information, but this right is balanced against other obligations — including medical-legal retention requirements. In practice, a patient's request to delete their records must be carefully assessed against the Health Professions Council of South Africa's retention guidelines and other applicable legislation.

TACFundi helps practices establish a clear, documented process for handling these requests — so your response is consistent, legally sound, and documented.
