What is POPIA and why does my practice need to comply?

The Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) is South African law that governs how organisations collect, store, process, and share personal information. It came into full effect on 1 July 2021 and applies to virtually every business in South Africa. Non-compliance can result in fines of up to R10 million per incident and potential criminal liability.

What happens if my practice is not POPIA compliant?

The Information Regulator of South Africa has the authority to issue enforcement notices, conduct investigations, and impose fines of up to R10 million per violation. Your compliance status is also publicly visible on the CIPC BizPortal — clients and referral partners can check it.

What is an Information Officer and do I need one?

Yes. POPIA requires every organisation that processes personal information to designate and register an Information Officer with the Information Regulator. TACFundi handles the registration process for you as part of our audit service.

What client data is covered by POPIA at a law firm?

Under POPIA, personal information includes names, ID numbers, contact details, financial information, and any information that can identify an individual. For a law firm, this covers virtually all client data: matter files, correspondence, property transfer documents, financial records, divorce proceedings, estate files, and more.

Does POPIA compliance affect how we use email?

Yes, significantly. Emailing sensitive client documents without encryption is one of the most common POPIA vulnerabilities we find at law firms. POPIA requires that personal information be protected with appropriate security measures during transmission — standard email does not meet this requirement for highly sensitive files.

How long does the POPIA audit take for a law firm?

For a typical small to mid-size law firm (2–25 attorneys), our initial audit is typically completed within 5–10 business days from engagement. We require a single information-gathering session and handle the rest independently. You receive a written compliance report, a priority action list, and a debrief session.

Why does POPIA treat medical practices differently?

POPIA classifies health information as "special personal information" under Section 26 — a higher protection category than general personal data. This means medical practices face stricter requirements, stricter enforcement, and higher penalties for violations than most other business types.

Does our practice management software need to be POPIA compliant?

The software itself needs to meet certain security standards, and your use of it needs to be properly governed. TACFundi reviews your practice management software setup as part of our audit and advises on any gaps — without requiring you to change systems unless absolutely necessary.

My receptionist handles most of the patient data. What do they need to know?

Quite a lot. Receptionists and admin staff need to understand what patient information they can share over the phone, how to handle requests for patient records, what to do if they receive a suspicious email or call, and who to contact if they suspect something has gone wrong. Our 90-minute training session covers all of this.
